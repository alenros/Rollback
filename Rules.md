# Rollback Colors: Quick-Start Rules
 
*A dice climbing game where beating a play means taking it back. Working draft for playtesting. 2–5 players, about 10–15 minutes per round.*
 
## Components
- **64 dice:** 4 colors (red, blue, green, yellow) × 4 types (d4, d6, d8, d12), 4 of each.
- **1 bag, 1 screen per player, and a score sheet.**
 
## Goal
Get out early. Players score by the order in which they empty their screens.
 
## Setup
1. Put all dice in the bag and mix.
2. Each player draws **9 dice** and rolls them behind their screen.
3. Dice keep their values. They are only rerolled when the rules say so.
 
## Plays
- **Single:** one die.
- **Set:** 2 or more dice showing the same value.
- **Run:** 2 or more dice showing **consecutive values** (for example, 5-6 or 3-4-5), any types.
- **Bomb:** either
  - a **same-color run of exactly 5** (for example, red 3-4-5-6-7), or
  - a **set of exactly 4 in four different colors** (for example, a red, blue, green and yellow 6).

A set or run whose dice are all one color is **one-color**. Color matters for bombs and for beating one-color plays.
 
## A trick
1. **Lead.** The leader plays **one die**.
2. **Beat, pass, or pick up.** Going clockwise, each player either beats the current play, passes, or picks up one die from the table and rerolls it. The turn keeps going around until the trick ends.
 
### Beating
- **A single** is beaten by a higher single, or by a set or run of **2 dice**. For example, a pair of 1s or the run 1-2 beats a single 12.
- **A set** is beaten by a stronger set, and **a run** by a stronger run. Stronger means **exactly one more die** (at any value), or the same number of dice with a **higher value**. To beat the run 9-10, play 10-11, 11-12, or a 3-die run such as 1-2-3.
- Sets and runs never beat each other.
- **A one-color set or run** can only be beaten by a **one-color** play. It can be any color: a blue pair beats a red pair, but a mixed pair doesn't.
- **A bomb** beats any single, set, or run, whatever its color. **You take nothing back:** the bomb and the dice it beat are removed from the round at once, the trick ends, and you lead the next trick.
 
You can never play more than **one extra die** on the table, except for a bomb. An equal play never beats (first played wins).
 
**When you beat a play, take the dice you beat behind your screen and reroll them**, unless you beat it with a bomb or with your last dice (see *Going out*). Your dice become the new play on the table.
 
### Passing
- Passing is **soft**: it doesn't lock you out. If the turn comes back to you, you can play or pass again.
- **When you pass, you may reroll up to as many of your dice as there are dice on the table.** Pass on a single to reroll 1, on a pair to reroll 2, and so on.
 
### Picking up
- Instead of beating or passing, you may **take one die from the play on the table** behind your screen, and **reroll it**.
- What's left on the table must still be a legal play, so you can take any die of a set but only an end die of a run. The table play stays with the player who made it.
- Picking up **counts as a pass** for ending the trick. If you take the **last die**, the trick ends and the **next player in turn leads** a single.

### End of the trick
When everyone except the last player to play has passed (or picked up) since that play, the dice on the table are **removed from the round**. That player leads the next trick. If they have already gone out, the next remaining player on their left leads.
 
## Going out
If a play uses **all the dice behind your screen**, you are **out** and take the next finishing place. You don't pick up the dice you beat; they leave the round. Your last play stays on the table, and the other players can still beat it as usual. The round continues until only one player is left, and that player finishes last.
 
## Scoring
 
| Finishing place | 2 players | 3 players | 4 players | 5 players |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| 2nd | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| 3rd | | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 4th | | | 0 | 1 |
| 5th | | | | 0 |
 
The first player out leads the first trick of the next round. For the first round, pick a starting player at random.
 
## Game end
Play **3 rounds**. The highest total wins. If tied, the player with more first places wins.
 
## Tips
- **Beating never makes your hand bigger.** You shed at least as many dice as you pick up. Playing more dice than are on the table, and above all bombs (5 dice out, nothing back), is how you actually get rid of dice.
- **Passing on big plays pays off.** Passing on a pair gives you two rerolls, but playing extra dice also hands everyone who passes more rerolls.
- **Rerolls build bombs.** A one-color 4-run is one die away from a bomb, and so is a three-color triple. Low values (1–4) can come up on every die.
- **Picking up is a targeted reroll.** It grows your screen by one, but it can hand you the color or value you're missing, and it weakens the play on the table.
- **Large dice make risky pickups.** A d12 you take back could come up 11 or 1.
- **Count screens.** Everyone's number of dice is visible, and so is every die type anyone picks up.
 
## Variants
- **Shorter game:** start with 7 or 8 dice.
- **Penalty scoring:** the round ends when the first player goes out. Everyone else scores penalty points for their leftover dice, and the lowest total wins. A face is worth the number of die types that can show it: 1–4 = 4, 5–6 = 3, 7–8 = 2, 9–12 = 1. This makes voluntary passing matter more.
